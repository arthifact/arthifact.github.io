In July 2025, during the [MIT Summer Geometry Initiative](https://sgi.mit.edu/sgi-fellows/sgi-2025-fellows/), I spent a week with Professor Eitan Grinspun and PhD student Jonathan Panuelo of the University of Toronto. The project built on a method that couples compressible fluids with thin deformable objects using moving Voronoi cells.

## Problem

In a moving Voronoi simulation, each particle carries a piece of fluid and owns one cell. The flow moves the particles, so some regions, like the wake behind an object at very high speeds, end up with large cells, and there the simulation loses detail. So the goal was to add detail in those regions without disturbing the rest of the flow.

## How it works

Instead of the full simulation, I built a small sandbox in Python to test one idea:

- **Find:** mark the cells that have grown too large.
- **Subdivide:** split only those cells into smaller ones, sharing the cell's mass, momentum and energy among them.
- **Leave the rest alone:** every other particle stays as it was, so the change stays local.
