From March to August 2026 I worked with Julien Chaput at the University of Texas at El Paso on a research project in full-waveform inversion (FWI), a way to image the ground from seismic recordings. My question was whether a few simple rules from statistics, signal processing and wave physics could decide how the seismic model is built, instead of a fixed grid.

## Problem

FWI sends sound waves into the ground, records the echoes at the surface, and searches for the velocity map whose simulated echoes match them. From a poor starting guess it lines up the wrong wave cycles and gets stuck. The usual fix is to start at low frequencies and build a smooth model first, but that smooth model is normally a pixel grid or a fixed layout of shapes, the same everywhere whether the geology needs it or not.

## How it works

I build the smooth model out of Gaussians, smooth bumps described by six numbers each. The model starts empty, and every ten steps a few rules decide what to add and what to remove:

- **Where:** a new Gaussian goes where the gradient asks for a change.
- **Shape:** it is stretched and tilted to follow that area, so it can follow a dipping layer.
- **Size:** it is never smaller than the data can see at that frequency.
- **Keep or remove:** it stays only if it improves the fit by more than a price set by the noise level.

After 9 Hz the smooth model is frozen, and ordinary pixel inversion adds detail up to 30 Hz.

## Results

I tested the method on four standard models (Marmousi, Overthrust, BP 2004 and SEAM) against a fixed grid of Gaussians and three pixel methods. The pixel methods stayed stuck near the starting guess. The adaptive model gave the best smooth model in most cases, even when the fixed grid got the same amount of computing, with the clearest win on Overthrust. On SEAM the fixed grid did better.

| Final error (m/s) | Starting guess | Best pixel method | Fixed grid | Adaptive |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Marmousi | 862 | 618 | 537 | **519** |
| Overthrust | 2,369 | 2,095 | 758 | **314** |
| BP 2004 | 1,530 | 1,141 | 446 | **437** |
| SEAM | 1,559 | 1,659 | **400** | 845 |
